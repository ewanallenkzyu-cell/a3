Frequently Asked Questions Regarding Bias Couture

Everything you need to know about the science of bias construction, fitting lead times, silk maintenance, and private consultations at our 181 Mercer Street atelier.

Standard planar garments are cut along the straight grain (warp) or cross grain (weft) of woven fabric, which possess zero natural elongation. A true bias cut rotates the pattern precisely 45 degrees. This diagonal orientation transforms the orthogonal textile weave into flexible diamond cells that expand and contract organically over bodily curves without requiring synthetic elastane or rigid internal boning.

Because the bias grain relies upon weave cell deformation under gravitational pull, newly assembled silk garments experience progressive elongation known as 'diagonal grain relaxation'. If a hemline is leveled immediately, it will distort unevenly over subsequent wearings. We suspend every commission on dress forms for 72 hours in climate-controlled conditions until grain drop stabilizes completely before leveling the hem.

Our standard couture commission requires three distinct private fittings at our 181 Mercer Street salon: the Muslin Toile Diagnostic, the Silk Suspension Review, and the Final Hand Finish Alignment. Standard lead time is eight to twelve weeks. Expedited commissions for international gala emergencies can be accommodated in four weeks upon request.

True bias garments must never be stored on vertical hangers for prolonged periods, as continuous gravitational tension will distort shoulder straps and lengthen the silhouette. We provide each patron with an acid-free archival textile box. Gowns should be loosely folded around acid-free tissue paper rolls and stored horizontally in a cool, moisture-stable environment.
